Sectors of the Indian Economy

JAIIB · Indian Economy & Indian Financial System · Module A

Chapter Summary

Imagine India’s economy as a chain: farmers and miners obtain natural resources (primary), factories and construction transform them (secondary), and banks, shops, transport and hospitals deliver services (tertiary). Research and knowledge-intensive activities are quaternary, while top-level policy and strategic decisions are quinary. India’s historical economic structure shifted from agriculture toward services, although agriculture remained important for employment. Green, White, Blue and other revolutions targeted specific agricultural products. Industrial output is tracked through IIP, while the eight core industries have their own index. Sunrise industries offer innovation-led growth; organised and unorganised work differ in registration, job terms and social protection. Distinguish employment share from output share, and historical figures from current estimates.

Important Concepts · Rapid Revision
  • Primary: extraction and farming; secondary: manufacturing, construction and utilities; tertiary: services.
  • Quaternary: knowledge and R&D; quinary: high-level decisions.
  • Green—foodgrains/Swaminathan; White—dairy/Kurien; Blue—fisheries; Yellow—oilseeds; Golden—horticulture.
  • Historical 2021–22 output shares: agriculture 18.6%, industry 28.6%, services 52.8%.
  • Cropping intensity = gross cropped area ÷ net sown area × 100.
  • IIP: mining, manufacturing, electricity; eight core industries include coal, steel and cement.
  • Organised enterprise and formal employment are related but distinct classifications.
