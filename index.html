Sectors of the Indian Economy

JAIIB • Indian Economy & Indian Financial System • Module A focus • Active recall practice

Chapter Summary
India’s economic activity can be understood as a chain that begins with natural resources, moves through processing and manufacturing, and then expands into services. The primary sector directly uses resources such as agriculture, forestry and fishing; the secondary sector processes raw materials through industrial activity; and the tertiary sector supplies services such as banking, education, healthcare and communication. Quaternary activity is the knowledge side of the economy—teaching, research and development—while the quinary sector covers high-level decision-making in government and private organisations. India’s structure has shifted over time: agriculture’s GDP share has fallen while industry and especially services have expanded, with services reaching 52.8% in 2021–22 in the supplied series. Agriculture nevertheless remains crucial for livelihoods and employment. Industry includes mining and quarrying, manufacturing, utilities and construction; the Index of Industrial Production (IIP), with 2010–11 as its base year, tracks production in mining, manufacturing and electricity. Services include trade, transport, finance, real estate, professional services and public administration. A sunrise sector is an emerging activity with strong growth and innovation potential. Finally, the organised sector is characterised by regular, regulated employment and social-security-type benefits, whereas the unorganised sector is dominated by small, scattered units and less secure employment. The key memory chain is: resource → processing → service → knowledge → decision.
Important Concepts — Rapid Revision
Primary: agriculture, forestry, fishing; direct use of natural resources.
Secondary: processing/manufacturing; industrial sector; construction included.
Tertiary: services such as banking, healthcare, education and communication.
Quaternary: “knowledge sector” — teaching, R&D and intellectual activity.
Quinary: highest-level decision makers in government/private sector.
GDP series: Services 33.3% (1950–51) → 52.8% (2021–22); agriculture 51.8% → 18.6%.
Industry: mining/quarrying, manufacturing, utilities, construction; IIP base year 2010–11.
Core industries: refinery products, electricity, steel, coal, crude oil, natural gas, cement, fertilisers.
Agriculture: 54.6% workforce (Census 2011 figure cited) and 17.8% GVA in 2019–20 at current prices.
Construction: second-largest employment sector after agriculture; over 31 million people and ~8% of total employment in the source.
Sunrise: emerging sector with high growth, innovation and public awareness; examples include Green Energy, FinTech and IT.
Organised: fixed/regular terms, stronger regulatory coverage, regular salary and employment benefits.
Unorganised: small/scattered units, irregular employment, weaker protection and often daily wages.
Memory chain: Green = Agriculture; White = Milk; Yellow = Oilseeds; Blue = Fish; Silver = Eggs; Golden = Fruits/Honey/Horticulture.
